### India 2023 Diary


#### June 21, 2023

![Flight to India](../../../img/flight-to-india.jpg)

Woke up by light and bird alarm at 5:30. Birds are soon off on their breakfast runs. Not able to get back to sleep even though I try. Finally wake up at 6:30. Shonu's alarm has gone off and she is awake too. Not enough milk so chai for Shonu as well today. I get Shonu to make paratha while I'm making chai and unloading dishwasher. Pancakes she has and I have a waffle. I cut apple and pack oranges and paratha for both of us. She gets ready before me. I'm ready by 8:10. We run through our checklist. Kamini calls. 

Pavan came at 8:45am and dropped us at airport by 10am. There is thick traffic. Along the way  pet horses flogged of capricious office managers and dysfunctional corporate America and greed. Gita pravachan is itching to emerge. Mercifully LAX is here. 

Check in is instant. No queue. Friendly lady attendant prints our boarding passes at kiosk and refers to Shonu as "your baby girl". 2 bags both weigh exactly 23kgs each. Bags dropped and lift taken to 4th floor for security check. Queue is there but not long and moves fast. Tray with epipen gets flagged as usual but released quickly. At terminal, Book Soup bookstore is entered and Benedict Society part 4 purchased for $10 since it isn't available through cheaper channels. Shonu suggests soup at Pret before the 15 minute walk to gate which is in another building. Tomato and feta cheese soup and bag of chips is polished off and long walk undertaken. 4 escalators later, our gate 204 is in sight. Delta DL8237/AF73 flight run by Air France. I return Pavan's call and regret it. Talk soon descends into politics and how one party is the scourge and another the savior led by the messiah. How easily brains are washed and narrative memes planted, like sheep led to pasture. Mercifully cut short by boarding announcement. 

Our seats 48A and B are at the very back. The last row. Back benchers. Shonu gives me hard stare. Nice thing is this row only has 2 seats.  Bathroom is right behind us. I rather like it. 

We both watch *Atrangi Re*. It is an offbeat theme about mental illness. Dhanush and Sara Ali Khan are marvelous. Akshay Kumar is competent. Don't have strong feelings one way or the other about his role. We should be seeing more of Dhanush in Bollywood. He is mega talented. Rahman of course is Rahman. A musical Midas. Our lunch is served first special Indian veg meal. There is a kind of chola/squash curry, boiled mung dal and rice. Nice. Bread I ask shonu to not eat. She has fruit and salad. She tells me later that she finished reading Benedict Society in the airport itself. She is a reading fiend. 

Some random observations:
	
The clouds divide the world into two. The world above them and the one below it. They bear no resemblance to each other. 
	
If someone from 100 years ago or 200 years ago were to sit in an airplane or even see an airport it would be utterly incomprehensible to them. Just as a future technology 100 years into the future would be for us. 

#### June 22, 2023

The approach to Paris was through a dense layer cake of clouds with flashes of earth far below. Disorderly fields and ponds 
seems in keeping with the French character which seems more freewheeling and disorganized than the Swiss and German machines. Some dramatic turbulence at fag end of flight. I felt reassured by watching an elderly bearded air host pushing a service cart who was totally unperturbed by it. In fact he rolled his eyes as if to mock it. He was like Captain Wolf Larsen in *Sea Wolf* riding a monstrous ocean without flinching. It was quite heroic. 

Lots of Africans and Middle Easterners in Paris airport. The legacy of a troubled colonial past. CDG airport appeared like it had seen better times. A torturous walk and airport train to get to the transfer terminal, followed by yet another tiresome security check with tiny red trays and an agent who made a big deal of the fact that we had 3 clear bags for liquids for 2 people and actually transferred the contents into 2 bags. Talk about mindless rule enforcement. As if having 2 instead of 3 bags somehow enhances safety. Lots of African and Indian staff in the airport. At the terminal, we are greeted by an ocean of desis and not a vacant seat in sight. We brush teeth and sit down in opposite terminal which has also been partially invaded by desis from the adjacent terminal. Remind me to never travel through Paris again. 

The desis have started lining up 1 hour before boarding time. It is a serpentine queue and the boarding at one point seems stalled for unannounced reasons. Finally we board and take our seats after a mini battle to find a luggage spot 
only for one fellow to try to squeeze in his suitcase into the same spot by reorienting ours so that the overhead bin wouldn't close. The air hostess tried to remove our suitcase at which I put my foot down and said that it was moved by someone. This individual then was forced to move their bag elsewhere. We are in a 3 seat row which isn't as convenient. 

The desis are fairly impolite when addressing the air hostesses. No "please", "can" or "could". We could collectively use lessons in etiquette as a nation, especially when traveling overseas. Each of us is an ambassador to the nation and such behavior shows us in poor light. 

An elderly uncle sits next to us, and I feel hemmed in. The lady in front has reclined her seat before the flight has even taken off. The joys of desi cotravellers. 

The plane waits for about an hour for conditions to clear up even though it isn't raining. It finally takes off one hour late. To my immense satisfaction, the air hostess has reprimanded the lady in front to put her seat upright. 

The flight is uneventful. Can't see that much on takeoff. Some suburbs and sections of the city are visible through openings in the clouds. Paris looks messy from the air, like a city that developed organically. No neat US like grids. Disappointingly we can't spot Eiffel Tower. A lunch is served soon after takeoff. Shonu is sleeping and refuses it. It is some dal palak and toor dal with Nan. Standard airline fare. Expected better from the French who are known for good cuisine. Shonu spends most of the time sleeping. I try and sleep in snatches of an hour at a time. Time passes slowly. 

I fill up immigration forms. All this information is available in the passport and boarding card which the immigration officer scans. Why burden passengers with this stupid form? Ditto for USA and it's ridiculous landing forms. 

Watched Hindi movie *Chehre*. Gripping story of retired lawyers in remote mountainous location who put unsuspecting passersby on mock trial, unearthing repressed crimes and delivering overdue justice. Big B, Anu Kapoor, Raghubir Yadav are all brilliant in their roles and even Emraan Hashmi isn't as intolerable as he usually is.

Watch *The Zen Diary* with Shonu. It is the diary of an old monk living alone in the hills, living off the land, cooking things he finds on his land and in the forest which vary with each season. In this way he is connected to the earth directly. His experience of the seasons is far more visceral than a city dweller's. The process of picking food, cleaning and cooking it is for him his path to nirvana. 

About 1.5 hours before landing a kind of light meal with a sandwich, berry yogurt and nuts is served. Shonu and me eat just the yogurt which is delicious and settles our stomachs after a day of airline food and sitting around. There is some cloud cover but we can spot lights on the approach. The front and bottom cameras make for an interesting new perspective. From the front camera, the red lights of the runway are visible from a distance like a beacon in the darkness. We finally touch down on Indian soil. Typically, the desis jump the gun and start pulling down luggage before the plane has parked and are reprimanded by the crew. I have a signal and text Appa to inform him. I see a message from him that he and Arjun are coming to pick us up. I'm very happy to hear that. 

Mother India. With all its flaws, one feels instantly connected and rooted as soon as one steps off the plane. As if one had never left. It is a feeling hard to verbalize. 

There are about 15 airport staff in the gangway as we exit the plane. Not sure why. Surely that many people haven't requested wheelchairs. 

Another annoyingly long walk to immigration counter where there is a small line moving at snails pace. The reason is the fingerprinting machine. Not sure why 2 biometric measures are required
facial scan and fingerprinting. No country I have been to requires fingerprinting to enter. Invariably people were having issues with the device not recognizing their fingerprints. It isn't a good introduction to the country for a visitor. The officer, a young chap, was friendly enough and it wasn't an issue for both me and Shonu. 

We picked up our bags ignoring touts whispering offers of assistance with getting bags through customs (an indication that corruption is still a problem) and exited. Took the lift to P10 and waited opposite Beer Cafe where Ola with Arjun and Appa arrived shortly. It felt great to meet both of them and especially good to see Appa at the airport after many years. To Arjun's credit he gave Appa confidence that he could come. 

We had a lively conversation on the way home and for some time thereafter on the monsoons, Utah trip, Shonu's hockey, vitamins vs fruits and vegetables. Apparently Arjun had to draw on his management skills to convince the cabbie to wait for us at the airport, since the pickup experience is abysmally designed with mercenary intentions. Kamini called and spoke for some time. Arjun left at around 4. Shonu and me washed up. I had a bath and turned in.

#### July 14, 2023

Entrepreneurs don't *own* 90% of the wealth of society. They *generate* that wealth through their ideas, efforts and risk taking. That wealth didn't exist a priori, on a tree somewhere waiting to be plucked. Certainly, nobody is claiming they did it all on their own. But they conceived of, funded and  organized the expedition. Those merely participating in the expedition do not have a moral claim on an equal share of the spoils for all their hard labor. They haven't invested risk capital. They will get paid a fixed amount, even as the business goes through ups and downs, provided they retain their jobs. In this way, a job is like a debt certificate rather than equity. The salaried always get paid first. Reward is always proportional to risk. Any investor will  tell you that lending to a person with a stable job versus a daily wage earner carries different risk levels and therefore different interest rates must be charged. Interest rate is another name for risk level. 

For all its lofty welfare goals, socialism is nothing but a parasite on free markets. Without the wealth that free markets generate, socialism has nothing to redistribute. Socialism fails to grasp human nature and motivation. Human beings are motivated by personal incentives, which socialism takes away and replaces with something grey and amorphous the welfare of the collective, the state. It asks human beings to work but donate the proceeds to this collective, who will then dole back out something which bears no proportion to the effort invested, but rather is based on need. The state arrogates to itself a moral role that most individuals do of their own accord donating to charity. Thus, a slouch with a large family gets a bigger slice of the pie than a genius with a small family or none, whose efforts have contributed far more towards the baking of that pie. Socialism is nothing but a demand for charity with a gun to your head that gun being the full power of the state apparatus. It turns human beings into parasites, constantly on the lookout for freebies and handouts, seeking to scrounge off the labor of their fellow men while expending the least effort themselves. It incentivizes parasitism. It destroys the very engine of wealth creation personal ambition. For that reason it is the greatest evil conceived of by the human mind. 

Caste is the greatest evil conceived of by the Hindu mind. It is the root of all problems that plague Indian society. As Ambedkar's metaphor brilliantly captures, it is a building with no stairs, elevator, entrance nor exit, with the residents doomed to spend their entire lives on the same level. I am ashamed to have been entirely oblivious to this aspect of Indian society until my current India visit and reading of *Annihilation Of Caste*. I am seeing my countrymen, especially the dispossessed, the oppressed millions with fresh eyes.

Her sir bought a new electric scooter.

#### July 20, 2023

Shonu learning ropes of *bhairavi*. She started singing *swar vistar* and Guruji corrected her. Gave her notes to write down. 

He is teaching *mian malhar* to another student. It was an awesome performance. Not sure how much the student learned though. He was only repeating simpler phrases. I suppose he is learning by listening. 

He is giving a talk on importance of having faith on something , somebody in life. This is the key to being good at anything. On enjoying singing in the present moment and not worrying about past or future. On how those who have risen to the top have intentionally designed their lives to keep sharpening focus towards their goals. Have surrounded themselves with people who help them move forward towards their goals. Have continuously discarded those things, people, situations that don't further their goal. Have separated social from personal relationships. Have only elevated those relationships from social to personal who help them in their quest and don't irritate or hinder them. Mastering Human relationships can accelerate progress towards your goals. Any personal development involves an iterative sharpening of focus from wider circles to narrower and narrower ones and finally to a single unshakable point. Once that state is reached nothing and nobody can shake you from achieving your goal. Request vs order — keep things at request stage. Avoid escalating from request to order just to satisfy ego. Having expectations leads us deeper into the web of entanglement. No reaction is the best action. 

This speech goes on for more than an hour. He even attempts psychotherapy. 

The whole speech is in Marathi. I tried using google translate but it won't auto translate someone giving a speech. Apple translate does, but it doesn't have marathi. Maybe I should make an app for this that does auto translate without having to keep pressing a mic button.

Now the kids are playing badminton. Looks like today is a light day. 

#### July 21, 2023

Shonu learning *kalashree* today. It is a combination of *kalavati* and *rageshree* her sir says it is the "marriage" of these ragas. Invented by Bhimsen Joshi. The gurukul is named after this raag and because he follows Bhimsen Joshi's style of singing. Lyrics: *dhana dhana bhaag suhaag tero, tu piya ke mana bhaaye.* *Dhana dhana Tansen, prabhu rijhaayi, tu hiii saban mai, det dikhaayi.* 

*Mian malhar*: he is saying taans need to be long to produce feel of rain clouds and thunder and expectation of heavy rainfall. 

He says it is a male raag or requires a heavy voice. Taans need to be in higher pitch since it is easier to take longer breaths. 

Shonu performance in gurukul is tomorrow at 6:30. She has to sing a set of *Durga, mian malhar, Bhairavi* and *payoji maine*. 

#### July 22, 2023

Shonu's concert is scheduled for 6:30pm today. Cook hasn't come. Made poha and ordered khichdi and aloo paratha from Haldirams. Paratha was spicy. Had it with yogurt. Khichdi was ok. 

Shonu watched *Pathaan* to relax after practicing. She had a bit of allergic congestion so told her to not strain her voice while practicing. 

Me and Shonu went to gurukul at 5:30 for practice. Shonu practiced her set with Atharva on tabla. She sounded pretty good. I was more nervous than her. Sir came briefly. They had prepared a stage upstairs and didn't want Shonu to see it. He had sent Kamu a pic. Sakshi practiced *raag yaman* after Shonu. She sang well but wasn't confident of pulling it off. 

We finally went up at 7. The stage was decorated nicely. A poster titled *Anubhav* was put up. I sat at the back after setting up my tripod. One of his students gave an introduction in English explaining the concept behind the *Anubhav* concert series as a platform for the younger generation. She gave a brief background of Shonu, messing up just once by using the word "great" instead of "grateful". To begin with, the chief guest, Ms Ashwini Jagirdar, an imposing elderly woman from some cultural association called *Sanskriti* was invited to present Shonu with a bouquet. I was presented with a bouquet as well for no good reason. Her sir then gave a speech in marathi giving Shonu's background as a different type of online student who has learnt from a great singer Arun Dravid ji from Jaipur gharana and now come to him for guidance. He also mentioned her dedication for learning and that she has come from US just for learning music. Her parents also came in for some praise for having rented a flat and brought her here. He took pride in the fact that Dravid sir's student was coming to him and to Kalashree gurukul for learning. 

The presenter then invited Sakshi to perform first. Kamini was texting me asking me to tell them to fix the camera angle and orientation for the Facebook live feed. It was too late since the performance had already started. I later saw her comments on the live feed where she was trying to get them to fix the feed 
she was remotely issuing instructions like a general. I don't think they saw the comments. 

Sakshi sang *raag yaman*. Atharva played tabla and Durva harmonium. She did a fairly good job though she didn't seem to go deep into the Raaga. Possibly she had a time limit to adhere to. 

Shonu took the stage next. Durva was replaced by Atharva on harmonium. Atharva was replaced by a tabla player named Sourabh, who had been specially asked to come for this concert. I was worried this would make Shonu nervous since she had only practiced live with either Atharva or her guru. 

She introduced herself and to my surprise spoke in Hindi though she had written her intro in English. She didn't show any nerves and even threw in the line about *chai nashta* that I had suggested. It elicited laughter and lightened the mood. She then gave an overview of the first raag she was presenting — *raag Durga*, *bada khyal* in *jhaptaal*, words are *Sakhi mori rooma jhooma*, followed by *chota khyal* in *teentaal* — *chatara sughara* and *tarana*.

Her voice rang loud and clear through the speakers. The sound was excellent. Her guruji said *wah* for the first note itself. Two senior citizens sitting next to me one of whom was guruji's father were appreciating Shonu's voice and singing. It was a remarkable performance given that this was her first classical concert discounting the competition she participated in at age 4. This was a proper concert and she nailed it. She wasn't just skimming the raaga's surface but trying to go deeper and explore it. Her guru was appreciating her performance from time to time and boosting her confidence. There was generous applause when she finished. 

Next she sang *raag Mia Malhar*. She started alaap with some *meends* which evoked appreciation. She did some long taans which her guru had said was how this *raag* had to be sung. It is a difficult *raag* to pull off and I had suggested to her to pick an easier one. But she told me she really enjoyed the *meends*. It was a fearless choice and she executed it well considering she only learnt the raag 3-4 days ago. This too received generous applause. 

Then she sang a bhajan – *payoji Maine Raam ratan dhan paayo.* She took a nice *alaap* as her sir had suggested. For this one, Divya ji replaced Atharva in harmonium. Shonu did a nice job. 

Finally she sang a traditional bandish in *raag bhairavi — Bhavani dayani*. This was also nicely rendered and there was thunderous applause at the end. I never knew Shonu was capable of singing like this. It was a pleasant surprise. 

Her guru gave a speech praising her dedication in learning and her courage in singing a difficult raag like *Mia malhar*. He said he was very happy to have a student like her and wanted more students like her. Kalashree was for students like her and she could come anytime and not only learn but also teach us. This was quite hyperbolic.  

Some of the older listeners also praised Shonu and her Hindi enunciation and Ashish ji for training her so well. One gentleman said her voice sounded like Sunidhi Chauhan singing classical music. Guruji's father was sitting next to me and congratulated me for the performance. 

Ashish ji asked Shonu to explain to the students about her preparation for the concert. Shonu *ashada vayinjeded* and said she didn't know what she did. All the students were magnanimous and congratulated her. When asked what she did for relaxation, I told them she watched *Pathaan*. This elicited laughter. Her guru remarked that now she seems like a 7th grader. 

Some group class students also congratulated her as we were waiting for Uber. 

We reached home at 9:15 and ordered pizza. I had asked Appa to have dinner so that he could have medicine. He had seen her performance on Facebook live and appreciated it. He went gaga in praising Shonu as usual. Tatha Patis also saw it live on Facebook.

Although fed up with India in monsoon, feeling very sad to leave Appa alone. Only positive is that he has Hari's visit in October to look forward to. Thoughts during the ride: Appa and Amma's soul is in India. For that reason it will always be close to my heart despite all the inconveniences. Take Uber and arrive at airport at 10:30. Business class check in is a breeze. No queue. We receive lounge passes. There is a separate security line for business class. Again minimal waiting although even here a woman is upset at non-business passengers sneaking in and delaying her by 2 minutes. The indignation is more for the invasion of a privileged space rather than for the delay I think. The immigration counter too is separate and very quick. 

We head to the Adani west lounge. Can't escape Adani nor his political patrons in India. Adani power gave us a very nice outage yesterday. And now the lounge which is admittedly quite posh. Feeling a little out of place here. We probably wouldn't rub shoulders with these types were we to live in Mumbai. There is a buffet and people are gorging themselves at midnight, because it's free food. We have a Sprite each — Shonu because she didn't finish hers in the fridge and I to settle my stomach. We while the time away by calling Kamini, then Appa, then Pavan and finally by playing Set. Of course Shonu wins. 

Feeling quite sleepy. We leave lounge at 1:45 and reach gate by 2am. There is a separate queue for business and first class. People have started queuing up but the queue is very small. We board at 2:30. Our seats are 5A and 5C on the left of the aircraft. There is no competition for overhead bin space. The seats are very comfortable with insane legroom. The seats can be adjusted electronically in all sorts of positions. It takes us a while to figure out how it works. The TV screen is huge and so far away that we can't reach it. We have to use a remote with a goofy pointing thing. There is a footrest that is nice and lots of storage in the front. They have provided a water bottle and a toiletries kit. There is a comfortable pillow and a thickish blanket. We are offered a welcome drink in a glass. We select orange juice. We feel pampered. This is a whole new universe we had only glimpsed but never experienced. I am very glad Shonu could get to experience this. Maybe if I had experienced this earlier, it might have motivated me to make more money. 

Takeoff is on the dot and very smooth though the drone of the engines is very audible since we are in the front. They have thoughtfully provided noise canceling AKG headphones which make things quieter. The Mumbai coastline and all my childhood memories recede behind us as we head out into the Arabian sea. It reminds me of the LA coastline except for the ubiquitous haze. 

I finally figure out how to make the seat completely horizontal. It is just a button touch. It is night and day in terms of comfort and we both have a nice 6 hour nap. 

About 1.5 hours before landing a breakfast is presented. Shonu wanted pancakes but the dough contains egg so she has to settle for the Indian vegetarian breakfast, which even I am beginning to tire of. Shonu sneezes a dozen times. Her allergies have kicked into high gear. I ask for black tea for her and for tea with cream for myself. The tea is served in a ceramic cup. It has a very nice Earl grey flavor. Shonu settles down a bit after drinking it. 

The breakfast is served in a larger tray with smaller plates atop a white tablecloth. There is a fruit plate which has papaya, watermelon, cantaloupe, grapes and pineapple. There is a plate with a sad looking half idli, a spinach cutlet, paneer bhurji and black Chana. The paneer bhurji is good. There is also hot plain paratha to go with it. Though I wasn't hungry I find myself eating. There is also a kheer like dessert which isn't that great. 

I watch *Bunty aur Babli 2* to while away time. It is fairly tolerable. Shonu is watching *Jayeshbhai Jordaar*.

Landing is equally smooth and on time. We exit as soon as the doors open. Business class is a gift that keeps giving. 

But now we are brought down from our high horse since the next leg is cattle class. No upgrade available. Not even to premium economy. Bummer. 

We take a train to terminal Z where our connecting flight is. On the way, we buy some chocolates for ourselves and friends and Shonu gets gum for herself. Once at the terminal, we my have to stand in a long security queue with desi hordes and screaming children. Shonu starts cribbing. She has gotten used to business class rather quickly. Even I am finding it a tough pill to swallow after all the pampering. 

The security check is taking inordinately long. Shonu's backpack is flagged for no good reason and it's contents inspected. They open and check books! That's a first. 

At the gate, we freshen up and inquire about upgrade but to no avail. 

Watched *Axone*. 

Landed ahead of schedule at 4:40pm in LAX. There were no forms to fill out which was a first. The walk to immigration has also been shortened. Immigration and baggage claim were quick. Apparently facial recognition is being used to eliminate forms but it brings with it privacy concerns. There was no customs line as well. This is the experience every country should be aiming for. We were out from aircraft to pickup spot in 40 minutes. Had to wait 20 minutes for Pavan to pickup. 

India themed discussions on way back with usual Muslim tropes resurfacing. Sad. Even Manipur is being made out as an illegal immigration and foreign hand conspiracy. Thankfully we reach home and the discussion has to end. 

At home, Kamu is coughing and wearing a mask. But happy to see us. We unpack a little, shower and do laundry. Shonu makes khichdi while I take bath. 

Call and speak to Appa. Turn in at 10:45. A long day spanning 3 continents and multiple time zones.
