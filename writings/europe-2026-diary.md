Our seats were at the back – 41A,B,C. There was no window to our shock. Which idiot designed this? It was slightly disorienting to not have the external reference frame as the plane taxied and took off but we were fine once in the air. 

Watched a movie, *The Taj Story*, featuring Paresh Rawal and some other well known character actors. I knew it was a propaganda movie but wanted to hear the arguments for myself. A long 5 minute disclaimer about the movie being a work of fiction with no intention of causing communal discord was read out at the beginning. It seemed disingenuous. He is utterly delusional and his book was filled with wild speculations confidently passed off as historical fact. Whatever "evidence" this movie presented, and as hard as it tried to present the appearance of being a balanced, neutral take on the controversy, only interested in setting the historical record right, it was clear to even the most gullible viewer that the maker had a clear agenda, and was riding the same wave that had brought the present ruling party to power. The sad thing is that many will fall for the argument without bothering to check facts or do their own research. That is what makes propaganda movies like this so diabolical. Were there kernels of truth in the movie? Sure. But they were nicely mixed up and served with lies and half truths.  

Dinner was served at around 6pm. Asian vegetarian cauliflower curry, palak, rice. Decent. Shonu was able to have it because it had allergen info. A nice kheer with some berry compote for dessert. This had no allergen info and Shonu avoided. 

Took a nap. One kid was screaming her head off nonstop for hours on end. Barely got any sleep. Woke up about 2 hours before landing when a breakfast was provided. A spinach paneer samosa and some fruits. The samosa was tolerable. 

#### Day 2: July 15 2026: Amsterdam

![Amsterdam](../../../img/amsterdam.jpg)
<span class="credit">Amsterdam</span>

Land on schedule at 12pm next day. Didn't see much from the air since no window. Schiphol airport is dated and not all that clean. Went through automatic passport control. Shonu went through. Both of our fingerprints failed to scan and we had to go through manual passport control. Shonu waited for us on the other side. The staff was very polite and chill about it. Took about 15 more minutes as the young officers were chatting away with each other and barely noticing the passengers as they stamped their passports. We walk through green channel as if strolling through a park. Nobody cares in stark contrast to say, Mumbai. A very chill and laid back airport experience. 

We walk out of the airport into what seems like an open air mall. In 2 minutes it leads to a train station. Schiphol station. The airport and train station are virtually joined at the hip. There are passport scanning kiosks here just like at the airport. This is for international travelers arriving or leaving by train. We have to tap Apple Pay to get into the station. Take Sprinter to Sloterdijk. Have to tap card out to exit station. Google Maps is having trouble orienting here but eventually we figure out the walking route to the left of the station. Then left onto an overbridge. Again Google Maps says go right but there is nothing there. Backtrack, get down from the overbridge and take a right on the street below to reach Hotel2Stay. Doors are closed and have to ring buzzer to enter. It is not yet 1pm, we are early and room isn't yet available. They let us check bags into cloakroom. Me and Shonu are suddenly hungry and we have one Kathi roll each. Aunty demurs and only has apple. It is slightly warm inside - the air conditioning is weak or there is none. Europe is very environment conscious and AC is not as ubiquituous as in the US. 

The Dutch are statistically the tallest people in Europe and the world. This apparently came about because the land reclaimed from the sea couldn't be used to grow vegetables but were excellent for growing grass which in turn enabled the dairy industry to flourish. Dairy is a huge part of the Dutch diet. 

Having time on our hands, we set out to find a grocery store. Go in one direction, but it's hot and windy and we have to walk 20 minutes. So backtrack towards the station. We notice that there is not much manicured landscaping along the sidewalks and between the streets. It almost seems unmaintained, unkempt even, though there are pretty flowers everywhere. Google search turns up that Amsterdam practices ecological landscaping, allowing native plants and weeds to thrive, including daisies, poppies and tulips, rather than mow and manicure frequently as is done in most cities. It makes for a unique cityscape, creating mini forests and wild patches that allow many bees, birds and local wildlife to flourish and soil to remain healthy. 

We find a Jumbo department store walking up the overbridge. Get some groceries – bread, butter, cheese, milk, croissants, strawberry yogurt, strawberries, biscoff butter, noodles, soup, chocolate, mocha sachets. The fruits and vegetables have a much more natural look and appear very fresh compared to the US. Most ingredients are in Dutch and Google Translate is invaluable here. 

Back at the hotel, we get our room number 112 after a few minutes of wait. It is a very spacious suite with a king bed, 2 single beds, a kitchenette and a dinner table and chairs. The only negative is that  the AC cannot be turned down very low, but it is at a comfortable setting. 

Feeling sleepy and hungry and need to take a shower as well. Quick shower,  another Kathi roll and then a long nap. Wake up by alarm at around 7. Wake up these two with great difficulty, luring them with mocha. 

Dress and leave for Central district. Take bus 22 from Sloterdijk station to Centraal station. A fantastic panoramic view as we exit the bus with the wide canal in the background. Walk to the central district with the iconic bridges and canal cruise boats. Photo ops abound at every corner. Keep walking, spotting the iconic guild houses to our left, where craftsmen and artists flourished in the 17th century. The streets are busy with foot traffic and surprisingly dirty. The people are fit and tall thanks to all that biking and walking. Cross to a shop selling fries. Get one small with ketchup. Fries are pretty good though a little messy. Go looking for vegan stroupwaffle shop Van Holland and a bookstore that Shonu bookmarked only to find both closed. Walk back, taking a tram to Centraal station, followed by train to Sloterdijk from platform 1. Reach back at 10:30. 

Noodles for dinner, chocolate for dessert. Turn in at close to 12. 

#### Day 3: July 16 2026: Amsterdam 

![Van Gogh Museum](../../../img/van-gogh-museum.jpg)
<span class="credit">Van Gogh Museum</span>

Wake up at 4:30am and can't go back to sleep so get up at 5:30am. Make mocha, have mozzarella and chips sandwich, and yogurt and strawberries. Get ready one by one and leave by 8am. Take 19 tram from Sloterdijk tram terminus to Rijksmuseum. There is a bicycle stand behind the tram station with hundreds of bicycles and people are parking their bikes and walking to the station. Bicycles and canals are the most recognizable features of Amsterdam. Walk 15 mins passing through Rijksmuseum to Van Gogh Museum which is a circular glass building. We reach by 8:45 and have timed tickets for 9am. Take a few photos outside and enter at 9, the first batch of visitors for the day. There is a massive two stories high screen displaying Van Gogh's works as we enter. Stow bags into locker which is locked by passcode and a symbol, for which we choose bicycle. 

Climb up stairs to 1st floor permanent exhibit called Van Gogh Face to Face consisting of his various self portraits. One thing that photos of artworks don't convey is the size of the work. These are all small to medium. Van Gogh did not paint many large canvases, if any. His self portraits are amazing, real works of art, with an ingenious technique of small multi colored brush strokes. He was doing selfies about 150 years before smartphones. 

Self portrait with grey felt hat is especially striking for its bold multicolor strokes and the piercing glare of the blue eyes. *Sunflowers, Montmarte, Almond Blooms*, are all famous and deservedly so. There is a section on how Van Gogh uses complementary colors and mixes colors, how he uses frames to create space. A couple of his paintings are put under a microscope and reveal interesting details such as sand grains on a painting made near a beach, and splotches on top of each other from working quickly. 

Van Gogh started as an artist only at the age of 27, having worked as an art dealer before that. He first learnt sketching and some of his sketches such as *Landscape in stormy weather* and *The Discus Thrower* are masterful. *The Sower* is sublime with its solar halo around the farmer's head. 

The museum was fairly empty when we arrived but now half of Beijing seems to have descended here. Surprisingly few desi faces. 

Upper levels have works by other artists who influenced and were in Van Gogh's circle such as Paul Gauguin. There is a work by Gauguin titled *Vincent Van Gogh painting sunflowers*, a tribute from one great artist to another. 

A temporary exhibition downstairs has more works. By this time we are saturated and exit. 

Stop at an Albert Heijn grocery right outside the museum to pick up orange juice, cheese, tortilla chips and Dutch chocolate. Pleasant picnic lunch at Vondelpark near a pond with ducks and pigeons. It's a 15 minute walk through quiet streets and we get a taste of the inner streets and how the locals live. We leave when the ducks get aggressive in approaching us for crumbs and Shonu is getting hyper about it.

Short walk to Rijksmuseum where we have a timed ticket for 1pm. Rijksmuseum is in an old building with impressively sculpted exteriors and grand interiors. Check bags at cloak room and take elevator to 2nd floor for Vermeer and Rembrandt. 

Start with Rembrandt's famous *The Night Watch* painting which seems to be undergoing restoration and is housed in a room behind a glass facade. Then we try to find more Vermeers and Rembrandts with little success until we employ the rule: *jahan gud wahan makhi*. Rembrandt's famous self portrait with its mastery of lighting, Vermeer's *The Milkmaid* are highlights.  I have a cappuccino and Shonu has an ordinary lemonade from a cafe near the exit. Go back to catch some more Van Goghs including a more famous self portrait. 

Walk through canal district to the same Stroopwaffle shop. The streets are buzzing with people and cyclists going at breakneck speed. They don't stop for pedestrians. We have to watch before crossing. It is a delightful walk with bridges over canals, and quaint shops. We even pass the consulate of Colombia. Amsterdam has a bohemian energy and charm that we haven't seen in other European cities. Although the accoutrements are modern, the spirit is very much enlightenment period. 

We stop at Vlaams Friteshuis Vleminckx, a famous fry shop, for fries along the way. These fries are crispier than the ones we had yesterday. This time we order with no sauce. 

Get vegan stroopwaffels from Van Holland Stroopwafels, the same shop that was closed yesterday. Along the way, a bookshop emerges serendipitously —The Old English books. Shonu is in ecstasy. It has a good curated collection. I get a Graphic Guide to Logic. Shonu gets a couple of novels —Babel and Percy Jackson. 

On the way back we see a canal cruise and buy tickets for it. It is a semi-closed boat with windows and an open roof. A bee has got in and is having trouble exiting. Shonu is in great tension. The bee is confused by the glass ceiling and tries to pierce it several times without success. Finally to Shonu's great relief it goes down and fortuitously back up a different path that takes it out. The cruise itself is relaxing, the captain is funny. We enter the canals after a while and get a different view of the buildings we walked past earlier. Some of the buildings are apparently tilting because the foundations are being rotted by saline water. The captain points out some very expensive properties where the monthly rent is nearly half a million euros. He shows us the sea locks and folding bridges and the mayor's massive house. Then we go out into a larger canal where large ships dock. This is the other side of the panoramic view we got from Centraal station. A cruise ship —Carnival— is docked. By this time we are exhausted. Shonu is sleeping. We are glad when the hour long tour finally ends. 

We take the tram back home. Tram 17 and then 19. 

Watching India vs England 2nd ODI. Willow refuses to load due to region restrictions even using VPN. Loads on crictime which is Willow's raw high quality feed without any ads. India bowling economically but not taking enough wickets reduces their chances. Not enough wicket takers. 

Tomato soup, cheese and puff pastry that aunty made in LA for dinner with Lindt chocolate dessert.

#### Day 6: July 19 2026: Brussels

![Herge Museum](../../../img/herge-museum.jpg)
<span class="credit">Herge Museum</span>

Visiting Herge museum today. Walk to Brussels Centraal station. We enter from the backside and there are no signs anywhere. After hovering around in confusion, we ask a gent who directs us and wishes us a good stay. Have to buy tickets from vending machine since this is intercity train. It is touch and go since the train arrives in 5 minutes. The vending machine is confusing but Shonu helps me figure it out and we rush to the platform. This is the train to Ottignies. It is a good thing we bought tickets else we would have had to leave the platform and find a ticket counter and come back, possibly missing the train. 

A 10-15 minute walk through a very large and quiet square, then a winding wooded path that leads to the museum. Tintin and Snowy welcome us inside. There is a model of the Thomson's car inside. Buy tickets and take elevator to 3rd floor. They have given a quiz to complete based on clues to be found in various exhibits. 

3rd floor has sketches of characters predating Tintin. Totor, a Boy Scout, Quick and Flupke, Jo, Zette and Jocko. All the experiments that culminated in Tintin. Hand drawn sketches of pages from TinTin books are instantly familiar and it is amazing to look at the original. Herge apparently loved cats yet gave TinTin a dog as companion. A history of Herge's life accompanied by his hand drawn sketches. Tintin first appeared in a serialized form in 1929 in a magazine called *Le Petit Vingtieme*. Interestingly, the character Chang who appeared in Tintin in Tibet, The Blue Lotus etc was a real person, a young artist and sculptor named Zhang Chongren, who Herge met in Brussels and who influenced his outlook towards life. Ironically, Herge never traveled to any of the foreign lands he depicted in minute detail. He got all his information from books, photographs and his own studies of these cultures. He was satisfying his own wanderlust through his books, and stoking the wanderlust of his readers. He did travel to America and Asia later in life. 

There are also character sketches of all the main characters in the novels. There is a model of the Nautilus submarine from Red Rackham's Treasure, model of the rocket from Destination Moon, the ship from The Seven Crystal Balls, a map of all the places Tintin has visited, a stunning cylindrical room with all the Tintin books in different languages arranged in columns, one column per langauge. It is almost 2:30 by now and we leave reluctantly after a brief stop at the gift shop. We have another place to go to.
